Task 1: Brylinski–Deligne, Weissman and Yifei Zhao — Covers of general reductive groups and the principal-block local Langlands correspondence

Learn and formalize the theory of covering groups for general reductive groups through Brylinski–Deligne central extensions, Weissman’s L-groups and Yifei Zhao’s étale metaplectic covers, and study the local Langlands correspondence (LLC) for the genuine principal block. Include local and adelic realizations and Iwahori/Hecke theory, with the field, base-scheme, coefficient, splitting and ramification hypotheses stated explicitly.

The core formalization targets are Brylinski–Deligne’s classification of central extensions by K2, Weissman’s construction of L-groups and parameters, and Yifei Zhao’s construction and classification of étale metaplectic covers and their dual data. The related work of Luozi Shi and Yifei Zhao on extended pure inner forms of covers provides further constructions and theorems on inner forms and Weissman’s obstruction to formalize.

Supplementary formalization targets include Gan–Gao’s treatment of the Langlands–Weissman programme and McNamara’s work on principal series and Satake theory.

Mathematical checkpoints

  1. Central extensions and K2 torsors. Definitions of central extensions, morphisms, pullback, pushout, Baer sum and splittings; the algebraic K-theory sheaf K2 and multiplicative torsors used in the sources. Formalize the stated equivalences between extension and torsor descriptions, retaining the field and base-scheme hypotheses.

  2. Classification of reductive extensions. The quadratic and lattice-extension invariants defined for tori, and the classification theorems for semisimple simply connected and general reductive groups. Formalize the reconstruction, morphism-compatibility and descent results proved in the sources.

  3. Étale metaplectic covers and dual data. Formalize Yifei Zhao’s description of covers by reduced étale 4-cocycles on the classifying stack of a reductive group scheme, their linear-algebraic classification and the associated Langlands dual data. Verify the functoriality and comparison results proved in that work for covers arising from Brylinski–Deligne extensions, with base-scheme and coefficient hypotheses explicit. Include the extended-pure-inner-form construction of Shi–Zhao and the stated results on Weissman’s obstruction, distinguishing the general obstruction calculation from the nonemptiness results proved for covering tori.

  4. Local and adelic realization. The Hilbert-symbol pushout of a given field-valued extension and the adelic restricted-product realization described in the sources. Formalize the proofs of exactness, continuity, the stated root-subgroup and integral splittings, and the rational splitting supplied by reciprocity, including the stated independence of choices.

  5. Dual groups and L-groups. Formalize the modified root datum defined in the cited literature and prove that its roots, coroots, lattices and pairings satisfy the root-datum axioms. Formalize the resulting complex dual group and L-group extension, including the Weil-group version and genuine character and gerbe/twist data. Verify well-definedness and formalize the source theorems on auxiliary choices, Levi compatibility and local/global constructions under their stated hypotheses. Record the proved ranges and distinguish conjectural extensions.

  6. Iwahori-fixed vectors. Work with a given Iwahori subgroup I, using the source’s definition, and with a splitting s over I when this is part of the hypotheses. Formalize the definitions of genuine representations and vectors fixed by s(I). Verify the source theorems on principal-series fixed spaces and intertwiner actions under their stated hypotheses.

  7. Hecke theory, Satake and LLC for the principal block. Definitions of genuine Iwahori and spherical Hecke algebras. Formalize their published presentations, actions on fixed vectors and Satake comparison with the modified dual group, together with the local Langlands correspondence established for the genuine Iwahori-spherical principal block. Retain the hypotheses and worked examples in the sources, including their splitting, tameness and ramification restrictions; identify conjectural extensions separately.

Each checkpoint contributes precise theorems, readable proofs, worked examples and checked Lean declarations using the common library definitions.

Core sources

  • Weissman, L-groups and parameters for covering groups, Astérisque 398 (2018), 33–186. DOI.
  • Yifei Zhao, Étale metaplectic covers of reductive group schemes, J. Eur. Math. Soc. (2025), published online first. DOI; arXiv.
  • Luozi Shi and Yifei Zhao, What are the extended pure inner forms of a cover?, preprint (2025). arXiv.

Supplementary reading

  • Gan–Gao, The Langlands-Weissman Program for Brylinski-Deligne extensions, Astérisque 398 (2018), 187–275. DOI.
  • McNamara, Principal Series Representations of Metaplectic Groups Over Local Fields, in Multiple Dirichlet Series, L-functions and Automorphic Forms, Progress in Mathematics 300 (2012), 299–327. DOI.
