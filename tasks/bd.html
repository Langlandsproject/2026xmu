Task 1: Brylinski–Deligne, Weissman and Yifei Zhao — Covers of general reductive groups and the principal-block local Langlands correspondence

Develop the theory of covering groups for general reductive groups through Brylinski–Deligne central extensions, Weissman’s L-groups and Yifei Zhao’s étale metaplectic covers, and study the local Langlands correspondence (LLC) for the genuine principal block. Include local and adelic realizations and Iwahori/Hecke theory, with the field, base-scheme, coefficient, splitting and ramification hypotheses stated explicitly.

The core formalization targets are Brylinski–Deligne’s classification of central extensions by K2, Weissman’s construction of L-groups and parameters, and Yifei Zhao’s construction and classification of étale metaplectic covers and their dual data. The related work of Luozi Shi and Yifei Zhao on extended pure inner forms of covers supplies a further direction concerning inner forms and Weissman’s obstruction.

Supplementary formalization targets include Gan–Gao’s treatment of the Langlands–Weissman programme and McNamara’s work on principal series and Satake theory.

Mathematical checkpoints

  1. Central extensions and K2 torsors. Define extensions, morphisms, pullback, pushout, Baer sum and splittings. Construct the algebraic K-theory sheaf K2 and multiplicative torsors. Prove the equivalence between the required extension and torsor descriptions, with field and regular-base hypotheses explicit.

  2. Classification of reductive extensions. Construct quadratic and lattice-extension invariants for tori; classify semisimple simply connected extensions. Assemble the quadratic form, lattice extension and compatibility map for general reductive groups. Prove reconstruction, compatibility on morphisms and effective descent to nonsplit groups.

  3. Étale metaplectic covers and dual data. Formalize Yifei Zhao’s description of covers by reduced étale 4-cocycles on the classifying stack of a reductive group scheme, their linear-algebraic classification and the associated Langlands dual data. Establish the construction’s functorial properties and its comparison with covers arising from Brylinski–Deligne extensions, with base-scheme and coefficient hypotheses explicit. Include the extended-pure-inner-form construction of Shi–Zhao and the stated results on Weissman’s obstruction, distinguishing the general obstruction calculation from the nonemptiness results proved for covering tori.

  4. Local and adelic realization. Push out field-valued extensions through Hilbert symbols. Prove exactness, continuity, root-subgroup splittings and the required integral splittings. Construct adelic restricted products and prove the rational splitting by reciprocity, including independence of auxiliary choices.

  5. Dual groups and L-groups. Construct the modified root datum, complex dual group and L-group extension over the Galois group, with its Weil-group version. Include the genuine character and gerbe/twist data. Prove compatibility with Levi subgroups, choices and passage between local and global fields. Establish the quasisplit construction and identify extensions beyond that range requiring proof.

  6. Iwahori-fixed vectors. Construct an Iwahori subgroup I and a splitting s where available. Define genuine representations, on which the central kernel acts by a specified faithful character, and vectors fixed by s(I); use genuine types in other cases. Compute principal-series fixed spaces and intertwiner actions under explicit hypotheses.

  7. Hecke theory, Satake and LLC for the principal block. Define genuine Iwahori Hecke algebras and prove their presentations and actions on fixed vectors. Develop the local Langlands correspondence for the genuine Iwahori-spherical principal block through its Hecke-algebra parameters. Construct spherical Hecke algebras and the Satake comparison with the modified dual group. Work out tori, GL(r), PGL(r), symplectic and nonsplit examples within the proved ranges, recording tame and ramification restrictions and separating established results from conjectural cases.

Each checkpoint contributes precise theorems, readable proofs, worked examples and checked Lean declarations using the common library definitions.
