Task 1: BD–Weissman — Covering theory for general reductive groups and LLC for the principal block

Develop the theory of covering groups for general reductive groups, starting from Brylinski–Deligne central extensions and Weissman’s L-groups, and study the local Langlands correspondence (LLC) for the genuine principal block. Include local and adelic realizations and Iwahori/Hecke theory, with the field, splitting and ramification hypotheses stated explicitly.

The main sources are Brylinski and Deligne for central extensions by K2; Weissman for L-groups and parameters; Gan and Gao for the Langlands–Weissman programme; and McNamara for metaplectic principal series and Satake theory.

Mathematical checkpoints

  1. Central extensions and K2 torsors. Define extensions, morphisms, pullback, pushout, Baer sum and splittings. Construct the algebraic K-theory sheaf K2 and multiplicative torsors. Prove the equivalence between the required extension and torsor descriptions, with field and regular-base hypotheses explicit.

  2. Classification of reductive extensions. Construct quadratic and lattice-extension invariants for tori; classify semisimple simply connected extensions. Assemble the quadratic form, lattice extension and compatibility map for general reductive groups. Prove reconstruction, compatibility on morphisms and effective descent to nonsplit groups.

  3. Local and adelic realization. Push out field-valued extensions through Hilbert symbols. Prove exactness, continuity, root-subgroup splittings and the required integral splittings. Construct adelic restricted products and prove the rational splitting by reciprocity, including independence of auxiliary choices.

  4. Dual groups and L-groups. Construct the modified root datum, complex dual group and L-group extension over the Galois group, with its Weil-group version. Include the genuine character and gerbe/twist data. Prove compatibility with Levi subgroups, choices and passage between local and global fields. Establish the quasisplit construction and identify extensions beyond that range requiring proof.

  5. Iwahori-fixed vectors. Construct an Iwahori subgroup I and a splitting s where available. Define genuine representations, on which the central kernel acts by a specified faithful character, and vectors fixed by s(I); use genuine types in other cases. Compute principal-series fixed spaces and intertwiner actions under explicit hypotheses.

  6. Hecke and Satake theory. Define genuine Iwahori Hecke algebras and prove their presentations and actions on fixed vectors. Construct spherical Hecke algebras and the Satake comparison with the modified dual group. Work out tori, GL(r), PGL(r), symplectic and nonsplit examples within the proved ranges, recording tame and ramification restrictions.

  7. LLC for the principal block. Specify the genuine principal block and its Langlands parameters and packets. Formulate the correspondence and its compatibility with Iwahori/Hecke and Satake theory, distinguishing established results from conjectural cases to be investigated.

Additional task: comparison with Kazhdan-Patterson covers

Identify the BD invariant triples giving KP covers of GL(r), with matrix size, covering degree and determinant twist as parameters. Prove local and adelic isomorphisms, including cocycle coboundaries, central embeddings, root lifts and compact/rational splittings. Transport induction, intertwiners and fixed-vector spaces with proved normalization factors.

This comparison is accepted separately from the KP main program. Cubic GL(2) is a worked specialization of the general comparison.

Each checkpoint contributes precise theorems, readable proofs, worked examples and checked Lean declarations using the common library definitions.
