Task 3: Bump–Ginzburg — Small representations, zeta integrals and automorphic liftings

Study the covering-group integral and lifting constructions of Bump–Ginzburg and their collaborators: theta and small representations, Fourier coefficients, local and global zeta integrals, symmetric-power L-functions, orthogonal-cover liftings and cubic Shimura periods.

The principal works are the Bump–Ginzburg symmetric-square construction, the Bump–Ginzburg–Hoffstein symmetric-cube construction, and the work of Bump–Friedberg–Ginzburg on cubic Shimura lifting, small representations and liftings between orthogonal covers.

Mathematical checkpoints

  1. Theta and small representations. The local representations and global Eisenstein residues defined on general linear, orthogonal and exceptional covers in the cited works. Formalize their stated irreducibility, Jacquet-module, constant-term, square-integrability and local/global compatibility results with the hypotheses for each family.

  2. Fourier coefficients and orbit support. Definitions of the local and global Fourier coefficients in the sources, through Jacquet modules and integration respectively. Formalize the vanishing and nonvanishing results determining nilpotent-orbit support, distinguishing minimal representations from other small representations.

  3. Models and uniqueness. The Whittaker, degenerate and other functionals defined for the periods and integrals in the cited works. Verify the stated dimension and induction results, and the uniqueness or nonunique-model arguments used to prove factorization.

  4. Local zeta integrals. Definitions of the local zeta integrals and functionals in the sources. Formalize their convergence proofs and unramified computations, together with the stated ramified and archimedean nonvanishing, normalization and intertwiner-compatibility results.

  5. Global integrals and L-functions. Use the measure conventions and definitions of global integrals in the sources. Formalize the convergence and unfolding arguments, Euler products and proved analytic consequences for the symmetric-square, symmetric-cube and other covering-related integrals. Identify the local-model and nonvanishing theorem used in each factorization.

  6. Liftings on orthogonal covers. The kernels and lifting operators defined using small representations in the cited works. Formalize the stated nonvanishing, cuspidality, Fourier-coefficient and local-compatibility results, with the source and image specified under the original hypotheses.

  7. Cubic Shimura periods. The period defined by pulling the minimal representation of SO(8) back along the adjoint map from PGL(3). Formalize the proved identities and their stated consequences for cubic Shimura lifting. Record the image conjecture separately from those theorems.

The local and global integral theories use the common representation and measure conventions. Each checkpoint includes a worked application, readable proofs and checked Lean declarations; useful Fourier and model lemmas become shared library results.
