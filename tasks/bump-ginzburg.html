Task 3: Bump–Ginzburg — Small representations, zeta integrals and automorphic liftings

Study the covering-group integral and lifting constructions of Bump–Ginzburg and their collaborators: theta and small representations, Fourier coefficients, local and global zeta integrals, symmetric-power L-functions, orthogonal-cover liftings and cubic Shimura periods.

The principal works are the Bump–Ginzburg symmetric-square construction, the Bump–Ginzburg–Hoffstein symmetric-cube construction, and the work of Bump–Friedberg–Ginzburg on cubic Shimura lifting, small representations and liftings between orthogonal covers.

Main theorem targets

  1. Symmetric-square integral representation. Formalize the main Bump–Ginzburg symmetric-square integral theorem: the stated global integral identity, its unramified local-factor calculation and the analytic consequences established in that paper, with its normalization and representation hypotheses.

  2. Symmetric-cube integral representation. Formalize the covering-group integral and its relation to the symmetric-cube L-function in Bump–Ginzburg–Hoffstein, together with the analytic assertions actually proved there. Retain the original field and automorphic-form hypotheses and distinguish those conclusions from later stronger results.

  3. Small representations of odd orthogonal covers. Formalize the main Bump–Friedberg–Ginzburg smallness theorems for the local and global representations of the specified odd-orthogonal double covers. The outcome is the stated nilpotent-orbit support, including the required vanishing and nonvanishing results; smallness does not identify every member of the family as a minimal representation.

  4. Automorphic lifting between orthogonal double covers. Formalize the lifting theorems in Bump–Friedberg–Ginzburg, from the specified genuine automorphic representations on even orthogonal double covers to the odd orthogonal double covers in that work. Preserve the source, image and hypotheses of each proved nonvanishing, cuspidality or Fourier-coefficient assertion.

  5. Proved period results for cubic Shimura lifting. Formalize the finite-field and Eisenstein-series results supporting the period criterion in Bump–Friedberg–Ginzburg. The general characterization of the cubic Shimura image by period nonvanishing remains a conjecture in that paper and is recorded as context.

Supporting material

Theta representations, Eisenstein residues, Fourier coefficients, local models, measures and unfolding lemmas support these integral and lifting theorems. Each definition or calculation is tied to the theorem that uses it, including the exact uniqueness or nonunique-model argument required for Euler factorization.
