Task 2: KP — Local and global metaplectic representation theory of GL(r)

Study the local representation theory and global automorphic theory of Kazhdan–Patterson (KP) covers of GL(r): covering tori, induction, exceptional representations, Whittaker models, Eisenstein series and metaplectic correspondence. Keep matrix size, covering degree and determinant twist as parameters; GL(2) is a worked example.

The foundational sources are Kazhdan and Patterson’s Metaplectic forms and their work towards a generalized Shimura correspondence, together with Flicker’s GL(2) theory and Flicker–Kazhdan metaplectic correspondence. The local programme also includes Zou’s correspondence results and the Kaplan–Lapid–Zou classification.

Mathematical checkpoints

  1. Concrete covers and covering tori. Construct KP cocycles, centers, splittings and torus representations in general rank. Determine genuine central characters and the restrictions to Borel and Levi subgroups.

  2. Induction and Jacquet modules. Construct metaplectic tensor products on Levi subgroups, normalized parabolic induction and Jacquet functors. Prove reciprocity, induction compatibility and the required geometric-lemma statements.

  3. Spherical and Iwahori theory. Specialize the common Hecke algebras to GL(r). Compute fixed vectors, Hecke actions and unramified parameters; prove the corresponding module and parameter identifications with splitting and tameness hypotheses.

  4. Intertwiners, exceptional representations and Whittaker spaces. Prove convergence, continuation, normalization and Weyl relations for intertwiners. Construct exceptional representations and determine the required Whittaker dimensions, including nonunique models.

  5. Characters, local correspondence and classification. Construct distribution characters and essentially square-integrable character correspondence. Prove Levi and induction compatibility and classify irreducibles from supercuspidal data using metaplectic tensor products and segments. Treat tempered classification and lifting uniqueness separately, incorporating corrected arguments.

  6. Local-field cases. Establish the required real, complex and wild-place results in their representation categories. Prove local normalization identities and their globalization consequences in the stated field ranges.

  7. Global automorphic theory. Construct adelic representations and Eisenstein series; prove constant-term formulas, continuation, functional equations and Maass-Selberg relations. Construct residues, determine Fourier coefficients with corrected normalizations, and derive the arithmetic applications over number and function fields in scope.

  8. Global correspondence. Develop orbital-integral matching, transfer and trace-formula comparisons in general rank. Prove lifting and multiplicity results with their representation restrictions and any unproved matching hypotheses explicit.

Additional task: comparison with BD realizations

Jointly prove the isomorphisms between concrete KP covers and BD realizations and transport the representation constructions through them. Accept this comparison separately; the main theory proceeds with the concrete covers.

Checkpoints include readable proofs, examples and checked Lean declarations.
