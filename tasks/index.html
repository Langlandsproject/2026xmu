AI-assisted learning and formalization

Learn and formalize the main theorems of covering-group theory and their proofs, with AI assistance. The definitions, constructions and supporting lemmas are introduced or reused as needed for those proofs.

Building a Lean-certified knowledge base

Vision

Build a mathematically accurate, general and extensible knowledge base, supported by Lean-checked proofs. It should help people understand the subject, use its results in their own work, and develop the theory further.

Requirements

  1. Main theorems and their proofs. Organize each task around the main theorems of the source theory, with each theorem’s reference, hypotheses, conclusion and proof explicit. Definitions, given data, constructions and auxiliary lemmas support these targets. Encode definitions directly and take the objects chosen or assumed in a theorem as input data. Formalize the source’s actual constructions and their well-definedness proofs where needed. A completed target includes the main theorem’s checked Lean proof, a readable mathematical proof, and reviewed agreement between the written and formal statements. Link the exact Lean declaration and record the checked revision, dependencies and axioms. Label conditional theorems and retain their assumptions; record conjectures separately as context.

  2. Generality. State definitions and results in the greatest generality supported by the arguments. Keep parameters explicit, identify where each assumption enters the proof, and derive important special cases from the general results.

  3. Consistent notation and conventions. Use a consistent set of notation and conventions throughout the exposition and Lean code. Explain how alternative formulations relate, including the equivalences or comparison maps needed to move between them.

  4. Reusable Lean interfaces and extensible type design. Build one coherent library on mathlib's types, structures and theorems, following its notation and API conventions. Choose general data structures and reusable interfaces that keep routine constructions and theorem applications simple. Use structures, typeclasses and explicit parameters according to their roles, keeping instance inference predictable and coercions coherent. Make multiple inheritance paths to the same structure agree definitionally to avoid conflicting instance diamonds. Check the design on representative examples and extensions.

  5. Explanations, examples and applications. Explain what the main definitions capture. Introduce the idea of a proof before its details and work out significant calculations. Include worked examples and applications showing how the results can be used in further arguments.

  6. Linked concepts and layered navigation. Make every mathematical concept in a statement clickable, with symbols and hypotheses linked to the definitions and conventions used there. Let readers move from a short explanation to full definitions, examples and prerequisite material. Provide direct links to proofs and Lean code. Show each page's place in the topic, its connections to other results and a clear route back to the original statement.

  7. Attribution, historical context and references. For important theorems, name the people who proved them and explain their place in the development of the subject, including later generalizations and corrections. Give full references with authors, titles, publication details, page or theorem locations, editions and DOI links. Identify contributors to the exposition and Lean formalization as well.

Tasks 1–4 below are learning and formalization goals, listed in decreasing order of priority.

Task 1: Brylinski–Deligne, Weissman and Yifei Zhao — Covers of general reductive groups and the principal-block local Langlands correspondence

Learn and formalize the theory of covering groups for general reductive groups through Brylinski–Deligne central extensions, Weissman’s L-groups and Yifei Zhao’s étale metaplectic covers, and study the local Langlands correspondence (LLC) for the genuine principal block. Include local and adelic realizations and Iwahori/Hecke theory, with the field, base-scheme, coefficient, splitting and ramification hypotheses stated explicitly.

The core formalization targets are Brylinski–Deligne’s classification of central extensions by K2, Weissman’s construction of L-groups and parameters, and Yifei Zhao’s construction and classification of étale metaplectic covers and their dual data. The related work of Luozi Shi and Yifei Zhao on extended pure inner forms of covers provides further constructions and theorems on inner forms and Weissman’s obstruction to formalize.

Supplementary formalization targets include Gan–Gao’s treatment of the Langlands–Weissman programme and McNamara’s work on principal series and Satake theory.

Main theorem targets

  1. Brylinski–Deligne classification theorem. Formalize the equivalence between the category of multiplicative K2-torsors on a reductive group and the category of quadratic-form, lattice-extension and compatibility data. The target is the categorical classification, including morphisms and descent, as in Theorem 7.2, with its regular-base hypotheses.

  2. Yifei Zhao’s étale classification theorem. Formalize the linear-algebraic classification of the 2-groupoid of étale metaplectic covers, including the torus case and the reductive-group-scheme statement in Theorem A. Retain the chosen Borel or torus, base-scheme and coefficient data required by the formulation, and the proved comparison with Brylinski–Deligne covers.

  3. L-groups and the proved parameterization theorems. Formalize the correctness and stated choice-compatibility of Weissman’s L-group construction for covers of quasisplit reductive groups, together with its proved parameterizations for split covering tori and unramified genuine representations. The root-datum axioms and gerbe constructions support these results; preserve the hypotheses of Weissman’s theorems.

  4. Principal-block Hecke correspondence and parameterization. Formalize the genuine Iwahori–Hecke algebra comparison theorems and the resulting correspondence for Iwahori-spherical representations in the cases treated by Savin and the supplementary sources. Make the source’s group, cover, field and splitting restrictions explicit, and identify exactly which Langlands-parameter statements follow in those cases. The spherical Satake parameterization is a related result for the smaller spherical subcategory.

  5. Inner forms and Weissman’s obstruction. Formalize the Shi–Zhao obstruction theorem as the extended pure inner form varies, and the proved nonemptiness and finiteness result for covering-torus packets when the obstruction vanishes. Keep the general obstruction result distinct from the covering-torus correspondence theorem and retain the preprint’s hypotheses.

Supporting material

Central extensions, K2 and étale cohomology, root data, the local and adelic realizations, genuine representations, and Hecke algebras are supporting material for these theorem targets. An Iwahori subgroup and any required splitting are defined or taken as given data according to the source. Their definitions, fixed-vector calculations and auxiliary lemmas are organized under the theorem whose proof needs them.

Gan–Gao and McNamara remain supplementary formalization sources. Their exposition and calculations can support the theorem targets without becoming independent foundational tasks.

Core sources

  • Brylinski–Deligne, Central extensions of reductive groups by K2, Publ. Math. IHES 94 (2001), 5–85. DOI.
  • Weissman, L-groups and parameters for covering groups, Astérisque 398 (2018), 33–186. DOI.
  • Yifei Zhao, Étale metaplectic covers of reductive group schemes, J. Eur. Math. Soc. (2025), published online first. DOI; arXiv.
  • Luozi Shi and Yifei Zhao, What are the extended pure inner forms of a cover?, preprint (2025). arXiv.

Supplementary reading

  • Gan–Gao, The Langlands-Weissman Program for Brylinski-Deligne extensions, Astérisque 398 (2018), 187–275. DOI.
  • Gordan Savin, On unramified representations of covering groups, J. reine angew. Math. 566 (2004), 111–134. DOI.
  • McNamara, Principal Series Representations of Metaplectic Groups Over Local Fields, in Multiple Dirichlet Series, L-functions and Automorphic Forms, Progress in Mathematics 300 (2012), 299–327. DOI.

Task 2: Kazhdan–Patterson — Representations, automorphic forms and metaplectic correspondence for GL(r)

Study the local representation theory and global automorphic theory of Kazhdan–Patterson covers of GL(r): covering tori, induction, exceptional representations, Whittaker models, Eisenstein series and metaplectic correspondence. Keep matrix size, covering degree and determinant twist as parameters; GL(2) is a worked example.

Treat Kazhdan–Patterson covers as a named family of Brylinski–Deligne covers in the library.

The foundational sources are Kazhdan and Patterson’s Metaplectic forms and their work towards a generalized Shimura correspondence, together with Flicker’s GL(2) theory and Flicker–Kazhdan metaplectic correspondence. The local programme also includes Zou’s correspondence results and the Kaplan–Lapid–Zou classification.

Main theorem targets

  1. Classification of genuine irreducible representations. Formalize the Kaplan–Lapid–Zou classification theorem: genuine irreducibles of the specified Kazhdan–Patterson covers are described by the appropriate multisegment data and compatible central character. Include the uniqueness, exhaustion and multiplicity-one assertions of the classification, with supercuspidal representations as its input data.

  2. Local metaplectic correspondence and its representation-theoretic consequences. Formalize the Flicker–Kazhdan character correspondence for the stated essentially square-integrable representations, using Zou’s corrected and extended results for Levi compatibility, parabolic induction and Jacquet modules. Include the proved reducibility criterion and the essentially square-integrable and tempered classification results in their precise ranges.

  3. Exceptional representations and metaplectic automorphic forms. Formalize the main local exceptional-representation results and global Eisenstein-residue theorems in Kazhdan–Patterson, using the published corrections. Target the proved representation-theoretic and Fourier-coefficient assertions, retaining the field, covering-degree and normalization hypotheses.

  4. Global metaplectic correspondence. Formalize the global correspondence results proved by Flicker and Flicker–Kazhdan, including the character and trace-formula identities used to establish them. Keep the stated automorphic-representation classes, central-character restrictions and multiplicity assertions attached to each theorem.

  5. Whittaker-dimension theorem: conditional formula and proved cases. Formalize the implication proved in Zou, §4, Theorem 4.7, retaining Conjecture 4.6 as an explicit hypothesis where it is used. Separately formalize the cases of that conjecture proved in §4.4, including the character and cuspidal cases specified there, and the resulting unconditional dimension statements.

Supporting material

The library definition of Kazhdan–Patterson covers, covering tori, metaplectic tensor products, induction, Jacquet modules, intertwiners and character theory support these theorems. Definitions and technical lemmas are introduced as required by their proofs. Archimedean or wild-place statements are included only when they occur in the cited theorem’s proved scope.

  • Kazhdan-Patterson, Corrections to Metaplectic forms, IHES 62 (1985), 203. DOI.

  • Kazhdan-Patterson, Towards a generalized Shimura correspondence, Adv. Math. 60 (1986), 161-234. DOI.

Task 3: Bump–Ginzburg — Small representations, zeta integrals and automorphic liftings

Study the covering-group integral and lifting constructions of Bump–Ginzburg and their collaborators: theta and small representations, Fourier coefficients, local and global zeta integrals, symmetric-power L-functions, orthogonal-cover liftings and cubic Shimura periods.

The principal works are the Bump–Ginzburg symmetric-square construction, the Bump–Ginzburg–Hoffstein symmetric-cube construction, and the work of Bump–Friedberg–Ginzburg on cubic Shimura lifting, small representations and liftings between orthogonal covers.

Main theorem targets

  1. Symmetric-square integral representation. Formalize the main Bump–Ginzburg symmetric-square integral theorem: the stated global integral identity, its unramified local-factor calculation and the analytic consequences established in that paper, with its normalization and representation hypotheses.

  2. Symmetric-cube integral representation. Formalize the covering-group integral and its relation to the symmetric-cube L-function in Bump–Ginzburg–Hoffstein, together with the analytic assertions actually proved there. Retain the original field and automorphic-form hypotheses and distinguish those conclusions from later stronger results.

  3. Small representations of odd orthogonal covers. Formalize the main Bump–Friedberg–Ginzburg smallness theorems for the local and global representations of the specified odd-orthogonal double covers. The outcome is the stated nilpotent-orbit support, including the required vanishing and nonvanishing results; smallness does not identify every member of the family as a minimal representation.

  4. Automorphic lifting between orthogonal double covers. Formalize the lifting theorems in Bump–Friedberg–Ginzburg, from the specified genuine automorphic representations on even orthogonal double covers to the odd orthogonal double covers in that work. Preserve the source, image and hypotheses of each proved nonvanishing, cuspidality or Fourier-coefficient assertion.

  5. Proved period results for cubic Shimura lifting. Formalize the finite-field and Eisenstein-series results supporting the period criterion in Bump–Friedberg–Ginzburg. The general characterization of the cubic Shimura image by period nonvanishing remains a conjecture in that paper and is recorded as context.

Supporting material

Theta representations, Eisenstein residues, Fourier coefficients, local models, measures and unfolding lemmas support these integral and lifting theorems. Each definition or calculation is tied to the theorem that uses it, including the exact uniqueness or nonunique-model argument required for Euler factorization.

References

  • Bump-Ginzburg, Symmetric square L-functions on GL(r), Ann. Math. 136 (1992), 137-205. DOI.
  • Bump-Ginzburg-Hoffstein, The symmetric cube, Invent. Math. 125 (1996), 413-449. DOI.
  • Bump-Friedberg-Ginzburg, On the cubic Shimura lift for PGL(3), Israel J. Math. 126 (2001), 289-307. DOI.
  • Bump-Friedberg-Ginzburg, Small representations for odd orthogonal groups, IMRN 2003(25), 1363-1393. DOI.
  • Bump-Friedberg-Ginzburg, Lifting automorphic representations on the double covers of orthogonal groups, Duke Math. J. 131 (2006), 363-396. DOI.

Task 4: Adams–Vogan–Trapa — Character lifting and unitary Shimura correspondences for real covering groups

Study the work of Adams, Vogan, Trapa and their collaborators on Shimura correspondences, character lifting and unitarity for real covering groups, including nonsplit real forms and complex groups viewed as real Lie groups. The task covers both the foundational results and the later small-representation and lifting problems described below.

The main sources are Adams–Barbasch–Paul–Trapa–Vogan for unitary Shimura correspondence over split real groups; Adams–Herb for character lifting; Adams–Trapa for nonlinear multiplicity duality; and Adams–van Leeuwen–Trapa–Vogan for unitarity algorithms. The later lifting questions include work of Tsai and the Tsai–Wong–Zhang preprint listed in the references.

Main theorem targets

  1. The proved unitary Shimura correspondence results. Formalize the Adams–Barbasch–Paul–Trapa–Vogan theorems: the injection from pseudospherical complementary series of the stated classical covers into the spherical complementary series of their associated linear groups, and the unitarity equivalence under Howe correspondence for the metaplectic symplectic representations containing the det^(1/2) K-type. The broader bijection is a separately stated conjecture in that work.

  2. Character-lifting theorem. Formalize the Adams–Herb theorem for admissible simply laced covering data: the lift of a stable virtual character is a genuine virtual character, possibly zero. Include the explicit stable-standard-module computation and the proved induction and infinitesimal-character formulas, under the source’s hypotheses.

  3. Nonlinear character-multiplicity duality. Formalize the Adams–Trapa block-duality theorem for genuine blocks of admissible two-fold covers at regular infinitesimal character, and its relation between standard and irreducible character multiplicities. Preserve the simply laced and real-form restrictions of the theorem.

  4. Signature and unitarity algorithm in its established setting. Formalize the signature formulas and correctness of the unitarity algorithm of Adams–van Leeuwen–Trapa–Vogan for linear real reductive groups. State that linear setting explicitly; application to a nonlinear cover requires a separately established theorem for that category.

Supporting material

The source definitions of real covers, Harish-Chandra modules, given K-types, standard modules, invariant Hermitian forms, transfer factors and Jantzen filtrations support these theorem targets. The auxiliary calculations are formalized within the proofs they serve.

The source-specific lifting theorems in Tsai’s work and the Tsai–Wong–Zhang preprint remain further reading, with their precise group hypotheses and preprint status. They do not replace the main published theorem targets above.

  • Adams-Trapa, Duality for nonlinear simply laced groups, Compositio Math. 148 (2012), 931-965. DOI.
