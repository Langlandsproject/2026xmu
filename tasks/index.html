AI-assisted learning and formalization

Learn the theory of covering groups through mathematical exposition, proof review and Lean formalization, with AI assistance.

Building a Lean-certified knowledge base

Vision

Build a mathematically accurate, general and extensible knowledge base, supported by Lean-checked proofs. It should help people understand the subject, use its results in their own work, and develop the theory further.

Requirements

  1. Mathematical statements, proofs and Lean certification. State each definition and theorem precisely, including its parameters, hypotheses and conventions. Give each theorem a complete readable proof and link its formal counterpart to the exact Lean declaration. A Lean-certified result has a completed, checked Lean proof and reviewed agreement between its written and formal statements. Record the checked revision, dependencies and axioms, and identify conditional results, conjectures and work in progress.

  2. Generality. State definitions and results in the greatest generality supported by the arguments. Keep parameters explicit, identify where each assumption enters the proof, and derive important special cases from the general results.

  3. Consistent notation and conventions. Use a consistent set of notation and conventions throughout the exposition and Lean code. Explain how alternative formulations relate, including the equivalences or comparison maps needed to move between them.

  4. Reusable Lean interfaces and extensible type design. Build one coherent library on mathlib's types, structures and theorems, following its notation and API conventions. Choose general data structures and reusable interfaces that keep routine constructions and theorem applications simple. Use structures, typeclasses and explicit parameters according to their roles, keeping instance inference predictable and coercions coherent. Make multiple inheritance paths to the same structure agree definitionally to avoid conflicting instance diamonds. Check the design on representative examples and extensions.

  5. Explanations, examples and applications. Explain what the main definitions capture. Introduce the idea of a proof before its details and work out significant calculations. Include worked examples and applications showing how the results can be used in further arguments.

  6. Linked concepts and layered navigation. Make every mathematical concept in a statement clickable, with symbols and hypotheses linked to the definitions and conventions used there. Let readers move from a short explanation to full definitions, examples and prerequisite material. Provide direct links to proofs and Lean code. Show each page's place in the topic, its connections to other results and a clear route back to the original statement.

  7. Attribution, historical context and references. For important theorems, name the people who proved them and explain their place in the development of the subject, including later generalizations and corrections. Give full references with authors, titles, publication details, page or theorem locations, editions and DOI links. Identify contributors to the exposition and Lean formalization as well.

Tasks 1–4 below are learning and formalization goals, listed in decreasing order of priority.

Task 1: Brylinski–Deligne, Weissman and Yifei Zhao — Covers of general reductive groups and the principal-block local Langlands correspondence

Develop the theory of covering groups for general reductive groups through Brylinski–Deligne central extensions, Weissman’s L-groups and Yifei Zhao’s étale metaplectic covers, and study the local Langlands correspondence (LLC) for the genuine principal block. Include local and adelic realizations and Iwahori/Hecke theory, with the field, base-scheme, coefficient, splitting and ramification hypotheses stated explicitly.

The core formalization targets are Brylinski–Deligne’s classification of central extensions by K2, Weissman’s construction of L-groups and parameters, and Yifei Zhao’s construction and classification of étale metaplectic covers and their dual data. The related work of Luozi Shi and Yifei Zhao on extended pure inner forms of covers supplies a further direction concerning inner forms and Weissman’s obstruction.

Supplementary formalization targets include Gan–Gao’s treatment of the Langlands–Weissman programme and McNamara’s work on principal series and Satake theory.

Mathematical checkpoints

  1. Central extensions and K2 torsors. Define extensions, morphisms, pullback, pushout, Baer sum and splittings. Construct the algebraic K-theory sheaf K2 and multiplicative torsors. Prove the equivalence between the required extension and torsor descriptions, with field and regular-base hypotheses explicit.

  2. Classification of reductive extensions. Construct quadratic and lattice-extension invariants for tori; classify semisimple simply connected extensions. Assemble the quadratic form, lattice extension and compatibility map for general reductive groups. Prove reconstruction, compatibility on morphisms and effective descent to nonsplit groups.

  3. Étale metaplectic covers and dual data. Formalize Yifei Zhao’s description of covers by reduced étale 4-cocycles on the classifying stack of a reductive group scheme, their linear-algebraic classification and the associated Langlands dual data. Establish the construction’s functorial properties and its comparison with covers arising from Brylinski–Deligne extensions, with base-scheme and coefficient hypotheses explicit. Include the extended-pure-inner-form construction of Shi–Zhao and the stated results on Weissman’s obstruction, distinguishing the general obstruction calculation from the nonemptiness results proved for covering tori.

  4. Local and adelic realization. Push out field-valued extensions through Hilbert symbols. Prove exactness, continuity, root-subgroup splittings and the required integral splittings. Construct adelic restricted products and prove the rational splitting by reciprocity, including independence of auxiliary choices.

  5. Dual groups and L-groups. Construct the modified root datum, complex dual group and L-group extension over the Galois group, with its Weil-group version. Include the genuine character and gerbe/twist data. Prove compatibility with Levi subgroups, choices and passage between local and global fields. Establish the quasisplit construction and identify extensions beyond that range requiring proof.

  6. Iwahori-fixed vectors. Construct an Iwahori subgroup I and a splitting s where available. Define genuine representations, on which the central kernel acts by a specified faithful character, and vectors fixed by s(I); use genuine types in other cases. Compute principal-series fixed spaces and intertwiner actions under explicit hypotheses.

  7. Hecke theory, Satake and LLC for the principal block. Define genuine Iwahori Hecke algebras and prove their presentations and actions on fixed vectors. Develop the local Langlands correspondence for the genuine Iwahori-spherical principal block through its Hecke-algebra parameters. Construct spherical Hecke algebras and the Satake comparison with the modified dual group. Work out tori, GL(r), PGL(r), symplectic and nonsplit examples within the proved ranges, recording tame and ramification restrictions and separating established results from conjectural cases.

Each checkpoint contributes precise theorems, readable proofs, worked examples and checked Lean declarations using the common library definitions.

  • Yifei Zhao, Étale metaplectic covers of reductive group schemes, J. Eur. Math. Soc. (2025), published online first. DOI; arXiv.

Task 2: Kazhdan–Patterson — Representations, automorphic forms and metaplectic correspondence for GL(r)

Study the local representation theory and global automorphic theory of Kazhdan–Patterson covers of GL(r): covering tori, induction, exceptional representations, Whittaker models, Eisenstein series and metaplectic correspondence. Keep matrix size, covering degree and determinant twist as parameters; GL(2) is a worked example.

Treat Kazhdan–Patterson covers as a named family of Brylinski–Deligne covers in the library.

The foundational sources are Kazhdan and Patterson’s Metaplectic forms and their work towards a generalized Shimura correspondence, together with Flicker’s GL(2) theory and Flicker–Kazhdan metaplectic correspondence. The local programme also includes Zou’s correspondence results and the Kaplan–Lapid–Zou classification.

Mathematical checkpoints

  1. Covering tori and genuine characters. Using the library definition of Kazhdan–Patterson covers, determine their centers, splittings and torus representations in general rank. Determine genuine central characters and the restrictions to Borel and Levi subgroups.

  2. Induction and Jacquet modules. Construct metaplectic tensor products on Levi subgroups, normalized parabolic induction and Jacquet functors. Prove reciprocity, induction compatibility and the required geometric-lemma statements.

  3. Spherical and Iwahori theory. Specialize the common Hecke algebras to GL(r). Compute fixed vectors, Hecke actions and unramified parameters; prove the corresponding module and parameter identifications with splitting and tameness hypotheses.

  4. Intertwiners, exceptional representations and Whittaker spaces. Prove convergence, continuation, normalization and Weyl relations for intertwiners. Construct exceptional representations and determine the required Whittaker dimensions, including nonunique models.

  5. Characters, local correspondence and classification. Construct distribution characters and essentially square-integrable character correspondence. Prove Levi and induction compatibility and classify irreducibles from supercuspidal data using metaplectic tensor products and segments. Treat tempered classification and lifting uniqueness separately, incorporating corrected arguments.

  6. Local-field cases. Establish the required real, complex and wild-place results in their representation categories. Prove local normalization identities and their globalization consequences in the stated field ranges.

  7. Global automorphic theory. Construct adelic representations and Eisenstein series; prove constant-term formulas, continuation, functional equations and Maass-Selberg relations. Construct residues, determine Fourier coefficients with corrected normalizations, and derive the arithmetic applications over number and function fields in scope.

  8. Global correspondence. Develop orbital-integral matching, transfer and trace-formula comparisons in general rank. Prove lifting and multiplicity results with their representation restrictions and any unproved matching hypotheses explicit.

Checkpoints include readable proofs, examples and checked Lean declarations.

Task 3: Bump–Ginzburg — Small representations, zeta integrals and automorphic liftings

Study the covering-group integral and lifting constructions of Bump–Ginzburg and their collaborators: theta and small representations, Fourier coefficients, local and global zeta integrals, symmetric-power L-functions, orthogonal-cover liftings and cubic Shimura periods.

The principal works are the Bump–Ginzburg symmetric-square construction, the Bump–Ginzburg–Hoffstein symmetric-cube construction, and the work of Bump–Friedberg–Ginzburg on cubic Shimura lifting, small representations and liftings between orthogonal covers.

Mathematical checkpoints

  1. Theta and small representations. Construct the required local representations and global Eisenstein residues on general linear, orthogonal and exceptional covers. Prove irreducibility, Jacquet-module and constant-term formulas, square integrability and local/global compatibility in the relevant families.

  2. Fourier coefficients and orbit support. Define coefficients attached to unipotent subgroups and characters, locally through Jacquet modules and globally through integration. Prove the vanishing and nonvanishing statements identifying nilpotent-orbit support, distinguishing minimal representations from other small representations.

  3. Models and uniqueness. Construct the Whittaker, degenerate and other functionals needed by periods and integrals. Determine their dimensions and behavior under induction. Prove the uniqueness or nonunique-model arguments that give factorization, using the shared representation-theory definitions.

  4. Local zeta integrals. Define the relevant local functionals, prove convergence and compute unramified factors in terms of representation parameters. Establish required ramified and archimedean nonvanishing, normalization identities and compatibility with local intertwiners.

  5. Global integrals and L-functions. Define quotient measures and global integrals, prove convergence regions and justify unfolding. Derive Euler products and the established analytic consequences for symmetric-square, symmetric-cube and other covering-related integral constructions. Track which local model and nonvanishing theorem supports each factorization.

  6. Liftings on orthogonal covers. Construct kernels from small representations and the resulting operators between genuine automorphic representations of even and odd orthogonal double covers. Prove their nonvanishing, cuspidality, Fourier-coefficient and local compatibility properties, specifying the source and image.

  7. Cubic Shimura periods. Construct the period obtained by pulling the minimal representation of SO(8) back along the adjoint map from PGL(3). Prove the established identities and evidence relating this period to cubic Shimura lifting; state the image conjecture and the exact consequences of later advances separately.

The local and global integral theories use the common representation and measure conventions. Each checkpoint includes a worked application, readable proofs and checked Lean declarations; useful Fourier and model lemmas become shared library results.

  • Bump-Ginzburg-Hoffstein, The symmetric cube, Invent. Math. 125 (1996), 413-449. DOI.
  • Bump-Friedberg-Ginzburg, On the cubic Shimura lift for PGL(3), Israel J. Math. 126 (2001), 289-307. DOI.
  • Bump-Friedberg-Ginzburg, Small representations for odd orthogonal groups, IMRN 2003(25), 1363-1393. DOI.
  • Bump-Friedberg-Ginzburg, Lifting automorphic representations on the double covers of orthogonal groups, Duke Math. J. 131 (2006), 363-396. DOI.

Task 4: Adams–Vogan–Trapa — Character lifting and unitary Shimura correspondences for real covering groups

Study the work of Adams, Vogan, Trapa and their collaborators on Shimura correspondences, character lifting and unitarity for real covering groups, including nonsplit real forms and complex groups viewed as real Lie groups. The task covers both the foundational results and the later small-representation and lifting problems described below.

The main sources are Adams–Barbasch–Paul–Trapa–Vogan for unitary Shimura correspondence over split real groups; Adams–Herb for character lifting; Adams–Trapa for nonlinear multiplicity duality; and Adams–van Leeuwen–Trapa–Vogan for unitarity algorithms. The later lifting questions include work of Tsai and the Tsai–Wong–Zhang preprint listed in the references.

Mathematical checkpoints

  1. Representations and operators. Construct real covers, genuine characters, compact types, Harish-Chandra modules and the required globalizations. Develop normalized induction, standard modules, intertwining operators and invariant Hermitian forms using common root-data and normalization conventions.

  2. Pseudospherical and Howe correspondence. Construct the associated linear group and parameter map; compute petite-type intertwiners and signatures. Prove the classical-group complementary-series injection and the symplectic unitarity equivalence for constituents containing det^(1/2). Distinguish these results from the broader bijection conjecture.

  3. Character lifting. For admissible simply laced covering data, construct transfer factors and lift stable virtual characters to genuine virtual characters, possibly zero. Compute lifts of stable sums of standard modules and prove the relevant induction and infinitesimal-character formulas.

  4. Blocks and multiplicity duality. Construct genuine Harish-Chandra blocks, standard/irreducible character expansions and Kazhdan-Lusztig-Vogan calculations. Prove character multiplicity duality for admissible double covers of simply laced real groups at regular infinitesimal character, including the allowed nonsplit forms.

  5. Small representations. Compute the lift of the trivial representation and identify its constituents, central characters and infinitesimal character rho/2, where rho is half the sum of positive roots. Prove the split simply laced result and examine nonsplit type D: the lift can be a proper subset of the small representations or zero. Treat the 2026 nonsplit result as a preprint target for proof review.

  6. Unitarity beyond principal series. Develop Jantzen filtrations, signature changes at reducibility points and the resulting unitarity calculations. Formalize the algorithm for linear real reductive groups and prove the extension lemmas for the nonlinear covering cases.

  7. Complex classical lifting. Study the Grothendieck-group lifts from SO(2r,C) to Spin(2r,C), and from Sp(2r,C) to Spin(2r+1,C). Verify the 2026 preprint's formulas for special unipotent sums and unitarity of their nonzero lifted constituents. This adds explicit families beyond the split-real pseudospherical setting.

Use common Cartan, parameter, character and Hermitian-form definitions throughout. Preprint results are targets for proof review.
