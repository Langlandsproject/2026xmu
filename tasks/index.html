AI-assisted learning and formalization

Learn and formalize the main theorems of covering-group theory and their proofs, with AI assistance. The definitions, constructions and supporting lemmas are introduced or reused as needed for those proofs.

Building a Lean-certified knowledge base

Vision

Build a mathematically accurate, general and extensible knowledge base, supported by Lean-checked proofs. It should help people understand the subject, use its results in their own work, and develop the theory further.

Requirements

  1. Main theorems and their proofs. Organize each task around the main theorems of the source theory, with each theorem’s reference, hypotheses, conclusion and proof explicit. Definitions, given data, constructions and auxiliary lemmas support these targets. Encode definitions directly and take the objects chosen or assumed in a theorem as input data. Formalize the source’s actual constructions and their well-definedness proofs where needed. A completed target includes the main theorem’s checked Lean proof, a readable mathematical proof, and reviewed agreement between the written and formal statements. Link the exact Lean declaration and record the checked revision, dependencies and axioms. Label conditional theorems and retain their assumptions; record conjectures separately as context.

  2. Generality. State definitions and results in the greatest generality supported by the arguments. Keep parameters explicit, identify where each assumption enters the proof, and derive important special cases from the general results.

  3. Consistent notation and conventions. Use a consistent set of notation and conventions throughout the exposition and Lean code. Explain how alternative formulations relate, including the equivalences or comparison maps needed to move between them.

  4. Reusable Lean interfaces and extensible type design. Build one coherent library on mathlib's types, structures and theorems, following its notation and API conventions. Choose general data structures and reusable interfaces that keep routine constructions and theorem applications simple. Use structures, typeclasses and explicit parameters according to their roles, keeping instance inference predictable and coercions coherent. Make multiple inheritance paths to the same structure agree definitionally to avoid conflicting instance diamonds. Check the design on representative examples and extensions.

  5. Explanations, examples and applications. Explain what the main definitions capture. Introduce the idea of a proof before its details and work out significant calculations. Include worked examples and applications showing how the results can be used in further arguments.

  6. Linked concepts and layered navigation. Make every mathematical concept in a statement clickable, with symbols and hypotheses linked to the definitions and conventions used there. Let readers move from a short explanation to full definitions, examples and prerequisite material. Provide direct links to proofs and Lean code. Show each page's place in the topic, its connections to other results and a clear route back to the original statement.

  7. Attribution, historical context and references. For important theorems, name the people who proved them and explain their place in the development of the subject, including later generalizations and corrections. Give full references with authors, titles, publication details, page or theorem locations, editions and DOI links. Identify contributors to the exposition and Lean formalization as well.

Tasks 1–3 below are learning and formalization goals, listed in decreasing order of priority.

Task 1: Brylinski–Deligne, Weissman and Yifei Zhao — Covers of general reductive groups and the principal-block local Langlands correspondence

Learn and formalize the foundational theory of covers of general reductive groups: the Brylinski–Deligne classification of central K2-extensions, Weissman’s L-groups, and Yifei Zhao’s enlarged framework of étale metaplectic covers. The goal is to formalize the main classification and correspondence theorems and their proofs.

The principal-block part concerns the established Iwahori-spherical correspondence and its Hecke-algebra description, within the group, cover and field hypotheses of the sources. Definitions and given objects are introduced as needed in those proofs.

Core references

  • Brylinski–Deligne, Central extensions of reductive groups by K2, Publ. Math. IHES 94 (2001), 5–85. DOI.
  • Weissman, L-groups and parameters for covering groups, Astérisque 398 (2018), 33–186. DOI.
  • Yifei Zhao, Étale metaplectic covers of reductive group schemes, J. Eur. Math. Soc. (2025), published online first. DOI; arXiv.

Further references

  • Luozi Shi and Yifei Zhao, What are the extended pure inner forms of a cover?, preprint (2025). arXiv.
  • Gordan Savin, On unramified representations of covering groups, J. reine angew. Math. 566 (2004), 111–134. DOI.
  • McNamara, Principal Series Representations of Metaplectic Groups Over Local Fields, in Multiple Dirichlet Series, L-functions and Automorphic Forms, Progress in Mathematics 300 (2012), 299–327. DOI.

Task 2: Kazhdan–Patterson — Representations, automorphic forms and metaplectic correspondence for GL(r)

Learn and formalize the local and global theory of Kazhdan–Patterson covers of GL(r), with irreducible-representation classification and metaplectic correspondence as the main goals. The foundational works are Kazhdan–Patterson on metaplectic forms, Kaplan–Lapid–Zou on classification, and Flicker and Flicker–Kazhdan on correspondence.

Kazhdan–Patterson covers are treated directly as a named family of Brylinski–Deligne covers in the library. The formalization follows the main theorems and proofs of the core papers, retaining their central-character, field and covering-degree hypotheses.

Core references

  • Kazhdan-Patterson, Metaplectic forms, IHES 59 (1984), 35-142. DOI.
  • Kazhdan-Patterson, Corrections to Metaplectic forms, IHES 62 (1985), 203. DOI.
  • Kaplan-Lapid-Zou, Classification of irreducible representations of metaplectic covers of the general linear group over a non-archimedean local field, Representation Theory 27 (2023), 1041-1087. DOI.
  • Flicker, Automorphic forms on covering groups of GL(2), Invent. Math. 57 (1980), 119-182. DOI.
  • Kazhdan-Patterson, Towards a generalized Shimura correspondence, Adv. Math. 60 (1986), 161-234. DOI.
  • Flicker-Kazhdan, Metaplectic correspondence, IHES 64 (1986), 53-110. DOI.

Further references

  • Zou, Local metaplectic correspondence and applications, Math. Z. 305 (2023), article 43. DOI.

Task 3: Adams, Barbasch, Paul, Trapa and Vogan — Unitary Shimura correspondences for real covering groups

Learn and formalize the unitary Shimura correspondence for split real groups developed by Adams, Barbasch, Paul, Trapa and Vogan, through harmonic-analytic methods and the comparison of representations of covering and linear groups. The main goal is to formalize the correspondence theorems proved in that work and their proofs.

The proved results include the complementary-series injection for the stated classical groups and the unitarity equivalence under Howe correspondence for the metaplectic symplectic representations containing the det^(1/2) K-type. Preserve these hypotheses and distinguish the broader correspondence conjecture. Character lifting, block duality, unitarity algorithms and later lifting results are provided below as further references.

Main reference

  • Adams-Barbasch-Paul-Trapa-Vogan, Unitary Shimura correspondences for split real groups, JAMS 20 (2007), 701-751. DOI.

Further references

  • Adams-Herb, Lifting of characters for nonlinear simply laced groups, Representation Theory 14 (2010), 70-147. DOI.
  • Adams-Trapa, Duality for nonlinear simply laced groups, Compositio Math. 148 (2012), 931-965. DOI.
  • Adams-van Leeuwen-Trapa-Vogan, Unitary representations of real reductive groups, Asterisque 417 (2020). DOI.
  • Tsai, Lift of the trivial representation to a nonlinear double cover, preprint (2020). DOI.
  • Tsai, Lift of the Trivial Representation: The Nonsplit Type D Case, preprint (2026). DOI, registration pending.
