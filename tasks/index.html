AI-assisted learning and formalization

Learn and formalize the definitions, constructions and theorems of covering-group theory from the cited literature, with AI assistance.

Building a Lean-certified knowledge base

Vision

Build a mathematically accurate, general and extensible knowledge base, supported by Lean-checked proofs. It should help people understand the subject, use its results in their own work, and develop the theory further.

Requirements

  1. Mathematical statements, definitions and proofs. Distinguish definitions, given data, constructions and theorems in the cited literature. Encode definitions directly and take the objects chosen or assumed in a theorem as input data. When the source actually gives a construction, formalize that construction and its well-definedness proof. Formalize the stated properties and theorem proofs with the original parameters, hypotheses and conventions. Provide a readable proof and link the exact Lean declaration. A Lean-certified result has a completed, checked Lean proof and reviewed agreement between its written and formal statements. Record the checked revision, dependencies and axioms; label conditional results and conjectures separately.

  2. Generality. State definitions and results in the greatest generality supported by the arguments. Keep parameters explicit, identify where each assumption enters the proof, and derive important special cases from the general results.

  3. Consistent notation and conventions. Use a consistent set of notation and conventions throughout the exposition and Lean code. Explain how alternative formulations relate, including the equivalences or comparison maps needed to move between them.

  4. Reusable Lean interfaces and extensible type design. Build one coherent library on mathlib's types, structures and theorems, following its notation and API conventions. Choose general data structures and reusable interfaces that keep routine constructions and theorem applications simple. Use structures, typeclasses and explicit parameters according to their roles, keeping instance inference predictable and coercions coherent. Make multiple inheritance paths to the same structure agree definitionally to avoid conflicting instance diamonds. Check the design on representative examples and extensions.

  5. Explanations, examples and applications. Explain what the main definitions capture. Introduce the idea of a proof before its details and work out significant calculations. Include worked examples and applications showing how the results can be used in further arguments.

  6. Linked concepts and layered navigation. Make every mathematical concept in a statement clickable, with symbols and hypotheses linked to the definitions and conventions used there. Let readers move from a short explanation to full definitions, examples and prerequisite material. Provide direct links to proofs and Lean code. Show each page's place in the topic, its connections to other results and a clear route back to the original statement.

  7. Attribution, historical context and references. For important theorems, name the people who proved them and explain their place in the development of the subject, including later generalizations and corrections. Give full references with authors, titles, publication details, page or theorem locations, editions and DOI links. Identify contributors to the exposition and Lean formalization as well.

Tasks 1–4 below are learning and formalization goals, listed in decreasing order of priority.

Task 1: Brylinski–Deligne, Weissman and Yifei Zhao — Covers of general reductive groups and the principal-block local Langlands correspondence

Learn and formalize the theory of covering groups for general reductive groups through Brylinski–Deligne central extensions, Weissman’s L-groups and Yifei Zhao’s étale metaplectic covers, and study the local Langlands correspondence (LLC) for the genuine principal block. Include local and adelic realizations and Iwahori/Hecke theory, with the field, base-scheme, coefficient, splitting and ramification hypotheses stated explicitly.

The core formalization targets are Brylinski–Deligne’s classification of central extensions by K2, Weissman’s construction of L-groups and parameters, and Yifei Zhao’s construction and classification of étale metaplectic covers and their dual data. The related work of Luozi Shi and Yifei Zhao on extended pure inner forms of covers provides further constructions and theorems on inner forms and Weissman’s obstruction to formalize.

Supplementary formalization targets include Gan–Gao’s treatment of the Langlands–Weissman programme and McNamara’s work on principal series and Satake theory.

Mathematical checkpoints

  1. Central extensions and K2 torsors. Definitions of central extensions, morphisms, pullback, pushout, Baer sum and splittings; the algebraic K-theory sheaf K2 and multiplicative torsors used in the sources. Formalize the stated equivalences between extension and torsor descriptions, retaining the field and base-scheme hypotheses.

  2. Classification of reductive extensions. The quadratic and lattice-extension invariants defined for tori, and the classification theorems for semisimple simply connected and general reductive groups. Formalize the reconstruction, morphism-compatibility and descent results proved in the sources.

  3. Étale metaplectic covers and dual data. Formalize Yifei Zhao’s description of covers by reduced étale 4-cocycles on the classifying stack of a reductive group scheme, their linear-algebraic classification and the associated Langlands dual data. Verify the functoriality and comparison results proved in that work for covers arising from Brylinski–Deligne extensions, with base-scheme and coefficient hypotheses explicit. Include the extended-pure-inner-form construction of Shi–Zhao and the stated results on Weissman’s obstruction, distinguishing the general obstruction calculation from the nonemptiness results proved for covering tori.

  4. Local and adelic realization. The Hilbert-symbol pushout of a given field-valued extension and the adelic restricted-product realization described in the sources. Formalize the proofs of exactness, continuity, the stated root-subgroup and integral splittings, and the rational splitting supplied by reciprocity, including the stated independence of choices.

  5. Dual groups and L-groups. Formalize the modified root datum defined in the cited literature and prove that its roots, coroots, lattices and pairings satisfy the root-datum axioms. Formalize the resulting complex dual group and L-group extension, including the Weil-group version and genuine character and gerbe/twist data. Verify well-definedness and formalize the source theorems on auxiliary choices, Levi compatibility and local/global constructions under their stated hypotheses. Record the proved ranges and distinguish conjectural extensions.

  6. Iwahori-fixed vectors. Work with a given Iwahori subgroup I, using the source’s definition, and with a splitting s over I when this is part of the hypotheses. Formalize the definitions of genuine representations and vectors fixed by s(I). Verify the source theorems on principal-series fixed spaces and intertwiner actions under their stated hypotheses.

  7. Hecke theory, Satake and LLC for the principal block. Definitions of genuine Iwahori and spherical Hecke algebras. Formalize their published presentations, actions on fixed vectors and Satake comparison with the modified dual group, together with the local Langlands correspondence established for the genuine Iwahori-spherical principal block. Retain the hypotheses and worked examples in the sources, including their splitting, tameness and ramification restrictions; identify conjectural extensions separately.

Each checkpoint contributes precise theorems, readable proofs, worked examples and checked Lean declarations using the common library definitions.

  • Weissman, L-groups and parameters for covering groups, Astérisque 398 (2018), 33–186. DOI.
  • Yifei Zhao, Étale metaplectic covers of reductive group schemes, J. Eur. Math. Soc. (2025), published online first. DOI; arXiv.

Task 2: Kazhdan–Patterson — Representations, automorphic forms and metaplectic correspondence for GL(r)

Study the local representation theory and global automorphic theory of Kazhdan–Patterson covers of GL(r): covering tori, induction, exceptional representations, Whittaker models, Eisenstein series and metaplectic correspondence. Keep matrix size, covering degree and determinant twist as parameters; GL(2) is a worked example.

Treat Kazhdan–Patterson covers as a named family of Brylinski–Deligne covers in the library.

The foundational sources are Kazhdan and Patterson’s Metaplectic forms and their work towards a generalized Shimura correspondence, together with Flicker’s GL(2) theory and Flicker–Kazhdan metaplectic correspondence. The local programme also includes Zou’s correspondence results and the Kaplan–Lapid–Zou classification.

Mathematical checkpoints

  1. Covering tori and genuine characters. Use the library definition of Kazhdan–Patterson covers. Formalize the source descriptions of their centers and splittings, genuine torus representations and central characters, and the stated restrictions to Borel and Levi subgroups.

  2. Induction and Jacquet modules. Definitions of metaplectic tensor products on Levi subgroups, normalized parabolic induction and Jacquet functors. Formalize their well-definedness and the reciprocity, induction-compatibility and geometric-lemma results proved in the sources.

  3. Spherical and Iwahori theory. The spherical and Iwahori Hecke algebras for the given covers of GL(r), with the fixed-vector spaces, Hecke actions and unramified parameters defined in the sources. Verify the stated module and parameter identifications with their splitting and tameness hypotheses.

  4. Intertwiners, exceptional representations and Whittaker spaces. The intertwiners and exceptional representations defined in the literature. Formalize the stated convergence, continuation, normalization and Weyl relations, and the Whittaker-space dimension results, including the cases with nonunique models.

  5. Characters, local correspondence and classification. Definitions of distribution characters and the essentially square-integrable character correspondence in the sources. Formalize the Levi-compatibility, induction and irreducible-classification theorems, using the stated metaplectic tensor products and segments. Keep tempered classification and lifting uniqueness distinct, and use the corrected arguments.

  6. Local-field cases. The real, complex and wild-place results established in the references, in their stated representation categories. Formalize the local normalization identities and globalization consequences with their precise field restrictions.

  7. Global automorphic theory. The adelic representations, Eisenstein series and residues defined in the sources. Formalize their constant-term formulas, continuation, functional equations, Maass–Selberg relations and Fourier-coefficient calculations with the corrected normalizations, together with the stated arithmetic applications and field ranges.

  8. Global correspondence. Formalize the orbital-integral matching, transfer and trace-formula comparisons established in the cited literature. Verify the proofs of the stated lifting and multiplicity results, retaining their representation restrictions and any conditional matching hypotheses.

Checkpoints include readable proofs, examples and checked Lean declarations.

  • Kazhdan-Patterson, Corrections to Metaplectic forms, IHES 62 (1985), 203. DOI.

Task 3: Bump–Ginzburg — Small representations, zeta integrals and automorphic liftings

Study the covering-group integral and lifting constructions of Bump–Ginzburg and their collaborators: theta and small representations, Fourier coefficients, local and global zeta integrals, symmetric-power L-functions, orthogonal-cover liftings and cubic Shimura periods.

The principal works are the Bump–Ginzburg symmetric-square construction, the Bump–Ginzburg–Hoffstein symmetric-cube construction, and the work of Bump–Friedberg–Ginzburg on cubic Shimura lifting, small representations and liftings between orthogonal covers.

Mathematical checkpoints

  1. Theta and small representations. The local representations and global Eisenstein residues defined on general linear, orthogonal and exceptional covers in the cited works. Formalize their stated irreducibility, Jacquet-module, constant-term, square-integrability and local/global compatibility results with the hypotheses for each family.

  2. Fourier coefficients and orbit support. Definitions of the local and global Fourier coefficients in the sources, through Jacquet modules and integration respectively. Formalize the vanishing and nonvanishing results determining nilpotent-orbit support, distinguishing minimal representations from other small representations.

  3. Models and uniqueness. The Whittaker, degenerate and other functionals defined for the periods and integrals in the cited works. Verify the stated dimension and induction results, and the uniqueness or nonunique-model arguments used to prove factorization.

  4. Local zeta integrals. Definitions of the local zeta integrals and functionals in the sources. Formalize their convergence proofs and unramified computations, together with the stated ramified and archimedean nonvanishing, normalization and intertwiner-compatibility results.

  5. Global integrals and L-functions. Use the measure conventions and definitions of global integrals in the sources. Formalize the convergence and unfolding arguments, Euler products and proved analytic consequences for the symmetric-square, symmetric-cube and other covering-related integrals. Identify the local-model and nonvanishing theorem used in each factorization.

  6. Liftings on orthogonal covers. The kernels and lifting operators defined using small representations in the cited works. Formalize the stated nonvanishing, cuspidality, Fourier-coefficient and local-compatibility results, with the source and image specified under the original hypotheses.

  7. Cubic Shimura periods. The period defined by pulling the minimal representation of SO(8) back along the adjoint map from PGL(3). Formalize the proved identities and their stated consequences for cubic Shimura lifting. Record the image conjecture separately from those theorems.

The local and global integral theories use the common representation and measure conventions. Each checkpoint includes a worked application, readable proofs and checked Lean declarations; useful Fourier and model lemmas become shared library results.

  • Bump-Ginzburg-Hoffstein, The symmetric cube, Invent. Math. 125 (1996), 413-449. DOI.
  • Bump-Friedberg-Ginzburg, On the cubic Shimura lift for PGL(3), Israel J. Math. 126 (2001), 289-307. DOI.
  • Bump-Friedberg-Ginzburg, Small representations for odd orthogonal groups, IMRN 2003(25), 1363-1393. DOI.
  • Bump-Friedberg-Ginzburg, Lifting automorphic representations on the double covers of orthogonal groups, Duke Math. J. 131 (2006), 363-396. DOI.

Task 4: Adams–Vogan–Trapa — Character lifting and unitary Shimura correspondences for real covering groups

Study the work of Adams, Vogan, Trapa and their collaborators on Shimura correspondences, character lifting and unitarity for real covering groups, including nonsplit real forms and complex groups viewed as real Lie groups. The task covers both the foundational results and the later small-representation and lifting problems described below.

The main sources are Adams–Barbasch–Paul–Trapa–Vogan for unitary Shimura correspondence over split real groups; Adams–Herb for character lifting; Adams–Trapa for nonlinear multiplicity duality; and Adams–van Leeuwen–Trapa–Vogan for unitarity algorithms. The later lifting questions include work of Tsai and the Tsai–Wong–Zhang preprint listed in the references.

Mathematical checkpoints

  1. Representations and operators. Definitions of real covers, genuine characters, compact types, Harish-Chandra modules and the globalizations used in the references. Formalize their normalized induction, standard modules, intertwining operators and invariant Hermitian forms, together with the stated well-definedness and compatibility results.

  2. Pseudospherical and Howe correspondence. The associated linear group, parameter map, petite-type intertwiners and signature calculations in the cited works. Formalize the classical-group complementary-series injection and the symplectic unitarity equivalence for constituents containing det^(1/2). Record the broader bijection conjecture separately.

  3. Character lifting. For the admissible simply laced covering data specified in the sources, formalize the definitions of transfer factors and character lifting. Verify the theorem that stable virtual characters lift to genuine virtual characters, possibly zero, and the stated standard-module, induction and infinitesimal-character formulas.

  4. Blocks and multiplicity duality. Definitions of genuine Harish-Chandra blocks, standard and irreducible character expansions, and the Kazhdan–Lusztig–Vogan calculations used in the literature. Formalize the character-multiplicity duality theorem for admissible double covers of simply laced real groups at regular infinitesimal character, within its stated real-form hypotheses.

  5. Small representations. Formalize the literature’s calculation of the lift of the trivial representation, including its constituents, central characters and infinitesimal character rho/2. Verify the split simply laced theorem and the stated nonsplit type D results, where the lift may omit some small representations or vanish. Retain the preprint status of the 2026 nonsplit work.

  6. Unitarity beyond principal series. Formalize the literature’s Jantzen filtrations, signature changes at reducibility points and unitarity calculations. Verify the unitarity algorithm for linear real reductive groups and the nonlinear covering cases established in the cited works, keeping their precise scope explicit.

  7. Complex classical lifting. Study the Grothendieck-group lifts from SO(2r,C) to Spin(2r,C), and from Sp(2r,C) to Spin(2r+1,C). Verify the 2026 preprint's formulas for special unipotent sums and unitarity of their nonzero lifted constituents. This adds explicit families beyond the split-real pseudospherical setting.

Use common Cartan, parameter, character and Hermitian-form definitions throughout. Preprint results are targets for proof review.
